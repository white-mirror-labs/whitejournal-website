30 Journaling Prompts for Self-Reflection

A good prompt does not tell you what to think. It gives your attention somewhere to start.

Use one prompt per sitting. Write for a few minutes without editing yourself. If a prompt does not land, skip it; another will.

Noticing how you feel

  1. What am I feeling right now? Where do I feel it in my body?
  2. What has my mood been like this week, in three words?
  3. What feeling have I been trying not to feel?
  4. When did I last feel completely at ease? What was around me?
  5. What is taking up the most space in my mind today?

Understanding what is behind it

  1. What happened just before this feeling started?
  2. What do I believe about this situation that might not be true?
  3. Which need of mine is not being met right now?
  4. If a friend described this to me, what would I tell them?
  5. What am I afraid would happen if I let this go?

Seeing your patterns

  1. What situation keeps repeating in my life?
  2. What do my best days have in common?
  3. What do I usually do when I feel overwhelmed?
  4. What do I postpone most often, and what does postponing protect me from?
  5. What did I learn about myself this month?

Relationships and connection

  1. Who do I feel most like myself around?
  2. What conversation have I been avoiding?
  3. Where do I say yes when I mean no?
  4. What would I like the people close to me to understand about me?
  5. Write a message you will not send.

Reframing

  1. What is another way to read what happened today?
  2. What is this difficulty teaching me?
  3. What am I being too hard on myself about?
  4. What would change if I assumed good intentions here?
  5. What part of this is within my control?

Choosing a next step

  1. What is one small thing I can do tomorrow that my future self will thank me for?
  2. What do I want more of this week? Less of?
  3. What would I do if I were not afraid of getting it wrong?
  4. What does a good enough day look like for me?
  5. Who am I choosing to become, one decision at a time?

Making prompts work

Prompts are most useful when you come back to them. Answer the same question a month apart and compare: the difference is often more revealing than either answer. If you are new to journaling, start with how to start journaling.

Common questions

How many prompts should I use at once?

One is enough. Depth on one question beats speed through many.

Can I repeat the same prompt?

Yes. Repeating a prompt weeks apart is one of the best ways to notice change.
